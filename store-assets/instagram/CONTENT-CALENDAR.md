## W3-C2 · A soft start-up, line by line (carousel · 8 slides)

**Concept.** Gottman's soft start-up framework, built as a real-time rewrite. This is the single most useful technique in couples research — Gottman found the first three minutes of a conversation predict its outcome 96% of the time.

**Slides:**

**1. Hook.**
> Your first sentence decides the next hour of your relationship.
>
> Here's how to build a soft start-up.

**2. The harsh version.**
> HARSH START-UP
>
> "You NEVER think about how tired I am. You just expect me to do everything."
>
> This is how 80% of fights begin. It's also why 80% of them end the same way.

**3. Rule 1.**
> RULE 1 · REMOVE "NEVER" AND "ALWAYS"
>
> These two words aren't accurate. They're weapons. They turn a moment into a verdict and put your partner on trial.
>
> Rewrite: "I'm feeling like my workload hasn't been seen lately."

**4. Rule 2.**
> RULE 2 · START WITH "I", NOT "YOU"
>
> "You" is an accusation. "I" is a disclosure. Your partner can argue with an accusation. They can't argue with how you feel.
>
> Rewrite: "I've been exhausted, and I don't think you've noticed."

**5. Rule 3.**
> RULE 3 · NAME A SPECIFIC MOMENT, NOT A PATTERN
>
> Patterns are debatable. Moments aren't. "You never help" starts an argument. "Last night when I came home and saw the dishes from this morning..." starts a conversation.
>
> Rewrite: "Last night when I came home and saw the morning dishes still in the sink, I felt really alone."

**6. Rule 4.**
> RULE 4 · ASK FOR SOMETHING CONCRETE
>
> Feelings without requests turn into complaints. Concrete asks turn into change.
>
> Rewrite: "Can we figure out a weeknight rhythm that doesn't leave me as the only person who notices the mess?"

**7. The finished soft start-up.**
> THE SOFT START-UP
>
> "Last night when I came home and saw the morning dishes still in the sink, I felt really alone. Can we figure out a weeknight rhythm that doesn't leave me as the only person who notices?"
>
> Same feelings. Completely different conversation.

**8. Closer.** (dark, with product motif)
> Hey Otis writes one of these with you, line by line.
>
> A private AI guide for couples. Four steps from rupture to repair.
>
> → JOIN THE WAITLIST

**Caption:**
A soft start-up isn't "being nice." It's a specific shape. Four rules:

1. No "never" or "always."
2. Start with "I", not "you."
3. Name a specific moment, not a pattern.
4. Ask for something concrete.

Get those four right and you've changed the next hour of your relationship before you've said the second sentence. Save this.

## W4-C1 · Anxious × avoidant, decoded (carousel · 7 slides)

**Concept.** The single most Googled dynamic in modern relationships. Save-bait for anyone in this loop.

**Slides:**

**1. Hook.**
> The most common fight in modern relationships is the same fight.
>
> One person reaches. The other pulls back.
>
> Here's what's actually happening.

**2. The loop.**
> THE ANXIOUS-AVOIDANT LOOP
>
> A reaches. B pulls back. A reaches louder. B pulls back further. Eventually A stops reaching. B wonders why.
>
> Nobody is wrong. Both are protecting.

**3. The anxious side.**
> IF YOU'RE THE ANXIOUS ONE
>
> The pull-back feels like abandonment. Your nervous system interprets distance as danger. The reaching isn't neediness — it's your body trying to re-establish safety. You're not too much. You need proof.

**4. The avoidant side.**
> IF YOU'RE THE AVOIDANT ONE
>
> The reaching feels like engulfment. Your nervous system interprets pressure as a threat to self. The pulling back isn't coldness — it's your body trying to regulate. You're not cold. You need space.

**5. What doesn't work.**
> WHAT DOESN'T WORK
>
> · Telling the anxious one to "calm down"
> · Telling the avoidant one to "open up"
> · Believing the loop is about love — it's about safety

**6. What does work.**
> WHAT DOES WORK
>
> Anxious → learns to self-soothe during the gap without abandoning the reach
> Avoidant → learns to reassure before retreating, even if it's just "I need an hour, I'm coming back"
> Both → learn to name the loop out loud

**7. Closer.**
> The goal isn't to stop being who you are.
>
> The goal is to recognise the loop — so you can step out of it together.
>
> Hey Otis helps both sides. Waitlist in bio →

**Caption:**
The anxious-avoidant loop is the most common pattern in modern relationships. One reaches. The other pulls back. The reach gets louder. The pull-back gets further.

Neither of you is wrong. Both of you are protecting.

Hey Otis speaks both languages. Vent separately. Understand separately. Come back together — with words that don't set off the other person's alarm.

## W7-C1 · The pursue-withdraw loop, decoded (carousel · 8 slides)

**Concept.** The single most common destructive pattern in couples research. Decoded as a flowchart-style carousel — one box per slide.

**Slides:**

**1. Hook.**
> Every couple gets stuck in this loop at least once.
>
> Most don't know they're in it.

**2. Step 1.**
> STEP 1 · A feels a gap
>
> Could be distance, quiet, a missed moment. A's nervous system reads "danger." A reaches out — usually with complaint, because that's what the feeling translates to.

**3. Step 2.**
> STEP 2 · B feels attacked
>
> B receives the complaint as an accusation. B's nervous system reads "overwhelm." B defends, deflects, or goes quiet.

**4. Step 3.**
> STEP 3 · A feels unheard
>
> The gap A was trying to close just got wider. A reaches out louder, harder. Now it's "you never" and "you always." A has become what B was afraid of.

**5. Step 4.**
> STEP 4 · B pulls back
>
> The withdrawal is no longer passive. It's protective. B goes quiet, or leaves, or turns on the TV. A feels abandoned.

**6. Step 5.**
> STEP 5 · Both feel wronged
>
> A: "You don't care enough to engage." B: "You don't care enough to ask gently." Nobody is lying. Both feel wronged. Both are.

**7. Step 6 — the exit.**
> STEP 6 · THE EXIT
>
> Either partner naming the loop out loud breaks it. "We're in it again. I'm going to pause before I make it worse. I'll come back." That sentence is the entire escape plan.

**8. Closer.**
> Hey Otis helps both partners see the loop — and step out of it at step 2, not step 6.
>
> Waitlist in bio →

**Caption:**
Every couple gets stuck in this loop at least once. Most don't know they're in it.

One person reaches with a complaint. The other receives it as an attack and withdraws. The reacher reaches harder. The withdrawer withdraws further. Both end up feeling wronged. Both are.

The exit is naming it out loud. "We're in it again. Let me pause before I make it worse."

# Production rhythm

## What's pre-designed (Figma — Social Media page)

- ✅ Post 8 · Post 5 · Post 3 · Post 1 (4 carousels, 22 slides total) — copy-amended
- ✅ 6 postcard carousel shells (Post 4 series, 12 slides)
- ✅ 4 story templates

## What needs to be designed per week

Each new carousel uses the same design pattern already in the Figma file:
- **Hook slide**: cream bg, eyebrow label (Inter Medium 22pt), big ink headline (Instrument Sans Bold 100–108pt)
- **Content slides**: cream bg, lime rule + eyebrow, big quote/line, annotation below
- **Closer slide**: ink bg, cream text, "WHAT HEY OTIS IS" block + 4-step motif + "JOIN THE WAITLIST"

Single-image tiles follow one of three patterns:
- **Big-quote tile**: cream bg, ink Instrument Sans Bold at 90–120pt, 1–2 lines max
- **Decode tile**: cream bg, italic intro at top, big hook quote, horizontal rule, 2–3 lime bullets
- **Dark quote tile**: ink bg, cream text, for contrarian / emphatic lines

You can batch all 32 assets over 3 sessions in Figma once per month.

## Workflow

- **Saturdays:** design next week's 4 posts + draft captions
- **Posting days (Mon/Wed/Fri/Sun, 7–9pm):** copy-paste caption, upload assets, drop hashtags in the first comment, reply to every comment for 60 min
- **Sundays:** check saves/shares per post. Note winners; adjust.

## Hashtag rules

- Never reuse more than 5 hashtags from your most recent post
- 5–7 "anchor" tags always in rotation: `#couples #couplestherapy #therapytools #softstartup #gottmanmethod #emotionalintelligence #heyotis`
- Vary the other 8–10 by post topic

## Save/share benchmarks

- Save rate >3% on at least one post per week — healthy
- Share-to-DM >0.5% on at least one per week — healthy
- Both under 0.5% for three consecutive weeks — rework the hook formula
